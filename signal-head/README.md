# 1/10 Scale Traffic Signal Head — LED Board

KiCad 10 project for the LED board behind a 1/10 scale 12 in (300 mm)
three-section signal head.

Generated by [`../gen_signal_pcb.py`](../gen_signal_pcb.py). Edit the
`VARIANTS` block in that script and re-run to regenerate — UUIDs are
deterministic, so regenerating does not churn the files.

```
python ../gen_signal_pcb.py ring      # this board
python ../gen_signal_pcb.py dense     # the 19-LED-per-lens variant
python ../gen_signal_pcb.py both
```

There is a second variant in [`../signal-head-dense/`](../signal-head-dense/):
57 LEDs, 19 per lens, filling the whole aperture. It is **not** an upgrade — it
targets a clear lens at near-zero standoff, where the dots are the visible
image, rather than a frosted diffuser. Behind a diffuser this ring board is the
better one; see below for why.

## Board

| | |
|---|---|
| Size | **35.6 × 106.7 mm** (1/10 of the 14 × 42 in signal face) |
| Lens centers | 17.8 / 53.4 / 89.0 mm from the top edge (35.6 mm apart) |
| LEDs | 36 × `LED_0805_2012Metric` (**KENTO KT-0805**), 12 per lens on an **11.5 mm** ring, front |
| Resistors | 36 × `R_0805_2012Metric` (**0805, 2.0 × 1.25 mm**), **6.0 mm ring, front** |
| Connector | J1, 4-pin 2.54 mm header, front, between the red and amber lenses |
| Mounting | 4 × M2 (2.2 mm), 3 mm in from each corner |
| Layers | 2 — the back carries nothing but the VLED pour |

**Everything that gets placed is on the front.** Single-sided SMD placement is
one assembly setup instead of two, which is the main cost lever at the fab.

Lit field is 27 mm across, inside a 30 mm lens. The dashed circles on
`Dwgs.User` mark the 30 mm lens apertures.

### Why the resistors sit *inside* the ring

The front has two free regions: inside the LED ring, and a thin strip outside
it. The interior is the roomy one — 12 × 0805 on a 6.0 mm ring leaves 1.24 mm
between courtyards and 1.57 mm clear of the LED footprints.

It also works out optically. The resistor bodies are dark and sit exactly where
a 12-LED ring over-lights (see below), so the slight shading nudges uniformity
the right way rather than the wrong one. At 0.45 mm tall and 15 mm from the
diffuser they cast no meaningful shadow.

### Why the LEDs are turned around

With resistors inboard, **the LEDs are rotated 180°** so pad 2 (anode) faces
the centre and pad 1 (cathode) faces out. Left in the default orientation the
anode would point outboard, away from its resistor, and every anode trace would
have to cross the cathode ring. Turned around, each anode is a straight radial
run on the front needing **no via at all**.

### Why the ring has no interior LEDs

A 12-LED ring does not leave the centre dark — all twelve emitters sit at equal
distance from it, so their cones pile up and the centre is the *brightest*
point. The deficit is at the lens **edge**. Modelling each 2835 as a Lambertian
emitter (`E = I0·h²/(h²+d²)²`) over the useful 24 mm aperture at this
housing's ~15 mm standoff:

| Layout | LEDs | Uniformity | Current @ 15 mA |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12 @ r=11.5 (this board) | 12 | **~86%** | 180 mA |
| 12 @ r=10 | 12 | 79% | 180 mA |
| 1 centre + 12 @ r=10 | 13 | 71% | 195 mA |
| 1 + 6 @ r=6 + 12 @ r=11.5 | 19 | 64% | 285 mA |

Every interior LED adds light where there is already too much. Filling the
array is **16 points worse** for 58% more current.

Two caveats: the model ignores the diffuser's lateral scattering and the white
cavity wall sitting exactly where the edge deficit is, so real edge falloff is
gentler than the table suggests. The conclusion about interior LEDs holds
either way, since it rests on the centre already being over-bright.

The exception is standoff. Below ~10 mm the donut becomes real — at h=8 mm a
r=10 ring drops to 69% with its minimum at the centre, and there a centre LED
does help. This housing is 17.8 mm deep with the board at the back wall, so it
sits comfortably in the ring-only regime.

If you would rather have the **visible dot-array look** of a real modern signal
module, that is a different optical design: clear or lightly tinted lens, near
zero standoff, LEDs as the visible image. That is the dense variant.

## Electrical

Common anode, low-side switched:

```
VLED (+9 V) --> R(n) --> LED(n) anode
                         LED(n) cathode --> K_RED / K_AMB / K_GRN --> switch --> GND
```

| Net | Nodes | |
|---|---|---|
| `VLED` | 37 | 36 resistors + J1.1 |
| `K_RED` | 13 | 12 cathodes + J1.2 |
| `K_AMB` | 13 | 12 cathodes + J1.3 |
| `K_GRN` | 13 | 12 cathodes + J1.4 |
| `A1`–`A36` | 2 each | one resistor to one LED anode |

Resistor values assume **15 mA per LED from a 9 V rail through a ULN2003A**
(about 1 V saturation drop):

| Color | Part | Vf | R | Actual | Per lens | Dissipation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Red, D1–D12 | KT-0805R | 2.4 V | 390 Ω | 14.4 mA | 173 mA | 0.080 W (64%) |
| Amber, D13–D24 | KT-0805Y | 2.1 V | 390 Ω | 15.1 mA | 182 mA | 0.089 W (71%) |
| Green, D25–D36 | KT-0805G | 2.9 V | 330 Ω | 15.5 mA | 186 mA | 0.079 W (63%) |

One resistor per LED rather than series strings: it keeps green alive as a 9 V
battery sags, is immune to Vf bin variation, and lets each color be trimmed
independently. The cost is efficiency — roughly 65% of the power goes into the
resistors.

### Why 0805 and not something smaller

| Package | Body | Rating | At 0.089 W |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1206 | 3.2 × 1.6 mm | 0.250 W | 35% |
| **0805** | 2.0 × 1.25 mm | 0.125 W | **70%** |
| 0603 | 1.6 × 0.8 mm | 0.100 W | 88% — too hot |
| 0402 | 1.0 × 0.5 mm | 0.062 W | 141% — fails |

Shrinking buys nothing here: the interior already has room to spare, and
package size does not change machine-assembly cost. Drop to 12 mA
(470 / 470 / 390 Ω) if you want more thermal margin, or 1206 if you want a lot.
The dense variant uses 0603 because it runs at 0.056 W *and* needs the board
area.

Only one color is lit at a time: **~186 mA peak** (green).

## Parts — JLCPCB

LEDs are the Hubei KENTO **KT-0805** family, chosen because all three colours
exist in one consistent series as JLCPCB **basic parts** (no per-part feeder
fee) with very deep stock. Put the `LCSC` field in your BOM column — that is
what JLCPCB matches on.

| Ref | Part | LCSC | Dominant λ | Iv @ 20 mA | Angle | Stock |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1–D12 | KT-0805R | **C2295** | 615–630 nm | 210 mcd | 120° | ~99 k |
| D13–D24 | KT-0805Y | **C2296** | 584–594 nm | 175 mcd | 120° | ~504 k |
| D25–D36 | KT-0805G | **C2297** | 513–528 nm | 430 mcd | 120° | ~1.6 M |

All three hit the wavelengths this design wanted (625 / 592 / 515 nm) and all
are 120°, which is the spec that actually matters behind a diffuser. The green
is a real 513–528 nm InGaN emerald, not the dim yellowish 565 nm GaP that cheap
"green" parts often turn out to be.

Resistors are plain 0805 thick film in 390 / 330 Ω — universally stocked basic
values, so just pick them in JLCPCB's parts selector.

### Is it bright enough?

At the operating currents above, per lens:

| | Iv per LED | 12 LEDs | Flux | Lens luminance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Red | ~151 mcd | 1.81 cd | 5.7 lm | ~2570 cd/m² |
| Amber | ~132 mcd | 1.59 cd | 5.0 lm | ~2250 cd/m² |
| Green | ~333 mcd | 4.00 cd | 12.6 lm | ~5670 cd/m² |

Luminance assumes a Lambertian 30 mm disc; a colour-matched diffuser will pass
roughly half to two thirds of that. For comparison a phone screen runs
500–1000 cd/m², so these read as bright indoors with margin to spare.

**Green is the odd one out** — more than twice the red, before accounting for
the eye being most sensitive in exactly that band, so it will look even more
dominant than the numbers suggest. If the three colours need to match visually,
raise green to **470 Ω** (10.9 mA, ~2.8 cd) or **560 Ω** (9.1 mA, ~2.3 cd) and
leave red and amber alone.

A caveat worth knowing: these are *indicator* LEDs. A 2835 lighting part such as
XINGLIGHT `XL-2835SURC-05` (C2843878, 6 cd) is far brighter, and that is what
the board was originally drawn for. The reason this design moved to 0805 is
that the 2835 family has **no verified amber** on LCSC — red and emerald green
are there, yellow/amber is not — and a traffic signal needs all three.

Both files were checked with `kicad-cli` from KiCad 10:

```
sch erc   ->  0 violations
pcb drc   ->  0 violations, 0 unconnected items, schematic parity 0
```

**This board is fully routed.** 116 track segments, 3 vias, and one filled
VLED pour, all generated by `route_inboard()` in the generator.

## Routing

Generated, not hand-drawn. Radii from each lens centre, everything on the front:

| | r |
|---|---|
| Resistor inner pad centers (VLED) | **5.0875 mm** |
| Resistor outer pad centers (anode) | 6.9125 mm |
| LED anode pad centers | 10.5625 mm |
| LED cathode pad centers | **12.4375 mm** |

Pads sit exactly on those radii, so straight segments between pad centers land
dead on every pad. Per lens:

- **VLED** — a 12-gon at r = 5.0875 mm through the resistor inner pads, a stub
  to the lens centre, and one via down to the back pour. Three vias serve the
  whole board; J1's VLED pin is through-hole and meets the pour directly.
- **Anodes** — a straight radial track from each LED anode pad to its
  resistor's outer pad, 0.3 mm wide. 2.675 mm of clear radial gap and **no via
  needed**, which is the payoff for turning the LEDs around.
- **Cathodes** — a 12-gon at r = 12.4375 mm, 0.5 mm wide. The chord between
  adjacent pad centers dips to r = 12.01 mm, still clear of the anode pads at
  11.05 mm.

The three cathode trunks run 0.6 mm wide out to J1. Red and amber reach it
straight across the inter-lens gaps; green has the amber lens in the way and
takes the clear channel by the right board edge (x = 83.5 mm, 2.1 mm in), which
also threads between the two right-hand mounting holes.

### Zone fill

[`../fill_zones.py`](../fill_zones.py) fills the copper pours via `pcbnew`, and
the generator runs it automatically after writing each board. This matters more
than it sounds: an unfilled pour carries no copper, so the three VLED vias read
as **dangling** and a fully routed board still reports unconnected items. If you
ever regenerate without KiCad's interpreter available, press `B` in pcbnew.

## What is left to do

1. **Set soldermask to white** when ordering. The board sits directly behind
   the diffuser and acts as a reflector — green mask absorbs light that white
   mask throws forward.
2. **Consider balancing green down.** See the parts section — green is the
   brightest leg by a factor of two before the eye's green bias is even
   counted.

## Fab notes

- 35.6 × 106.7 mm **exceeds the 100 × 100 mm budget tier** at most fabs. This
  is the exact 1/10 scale; shrink `BOARD_H` to 99 mm in the generator if you
  would rather stay in the cheap tier.
- Reference designators on D*, R*, and H* are hidden on silkscreen — 72 parts
  on 6 and 11.5 mm rings makes them unreadable. Designators remain on F.Fab for
  assembly. J1 keeps a visible label, and each lens is captioned on B.SilkS with
  its color and resistor value.
- **72 placements, all on one side.** The dense variant is 114.

## Firmware

[`../firmware/signal_head/signal_head.ino`](../firmware/signal_head/signal_head.ino)
drives this board through a ULN2003A and adds a mode button on D5:

| Mode | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| `MODE_CYCLE` | normal green -> amber -> red cycle |
| `MODE_RED` | red ring locked on |
| `MODE_AMB` | amber ring locked on |
| `MODE_GRN` | green ring locked on |

Each press steps to the next mode and wraps. The button is a momentary switch
from D5 to GND using the internal pull-up — it lives with the microcontroller,
not on this board, so no respin is needed.
